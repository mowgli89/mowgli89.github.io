How mechanical force shapes the fetal lung.

We work across the laboratory and the operating room to understand how mechanical force drives fetal lung development, why that development goes wrong in congenital diaphragmatic hernia, and how fetal intervention can change the outcome.

Research Mechanobiology of the fetal lung

What we study.

The developing lung grows on a fixed schedule. Physical forces around the lung such as in congenital diaphragmatic hernia can interrupt normal development. Our lab studies how force and form are linked, and where an intervention can move that development back toward normal.

Mechanosensing

PIEZO1 to calcium to YAP

We follow how alveolar epithelial cells react to mechanical stretch to alter their developmental fate. We are interested in specific mechanoreceptors and how they transcriptionally regulate processes that contribute to cell fate.

FETO as a biological signal

dose and duration, not a switch

We use fetal tracheal occlusion, an existing fetal therapy, as a model of force modulation. We are interested in the application of mechanical alveolar distension as a dosable stimulus that can affect how different babies with similar anatomy can respond to the same operation in different ways.

From human tissue to working model

stretch, slices, spatial data

We test these ideas in human neonatal lung cells held under controlled stretch, in precision-cut lung slices that keep the tissue's architecture intact, and in spatial multiomic assessment of lung tissue.

The principal investigator

Sourav K. Bose, MD, MBA, MSc

Sourav is a pediatric surgeon and surgeon-scientist who directs the Bose FABLE Lab. He is an Assistant Professor of Surgery at Baylor College of Medicine and a pediatric surgeon at Texas Children's Hospital, affiliated with the Texas Children's Fetal Center.

His program runs across the operating room and the bench, centered on how the developing lung senses mechanical force and on how fetal interventions such as tracheal occlusion can be understood well enough to be improved.

He trained in general surgery at Brigham and Women's Hospital, completed a fetal research fellowship at the Children's Hospital of Philadelphia Center for Fetal Research, and finished his pediatric surgery fellowship at Texas Children's Hospital. He earned his MD from the Perelman School of Medicine, an MBA from the Wharton School, and an MSc from the London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine, and as an undergraduate he studied in the Vagelos Program in Life Sciences and Management at the University of Pennsylvania.
